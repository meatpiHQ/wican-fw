# The repair hammer

Command: `python tools\testbench\usb\espnetlink_repair_hammer.py [--n 8] [--spacing 20] [--server betty\|none]`

Needs: DUT at home on the bench AP + the dongle on the connector in a USB mode + rpi001 + betty

Moved from the `TESTING.md` test-kinds table on 2026-10-31 (the row's detail cell, whole, as paragraphs). The index row and the other stages: [../TESTING.md](../TESTING.md).

**The repair hammer** (→ `ESPNL REPAIR HAMMER PASS`, ~8 min for 8 cycles): `POST /api/espnetlink/repair` (a VBUS cycle of the ESPNetLink) again and again while the wire carries traffic, and the WiCAN must never reset. Written 2026-10-12 for the crash the garage bench's g6 leg found (`abort: assert failed: spinlock_acquire ... task "usbh_rndis_rx"`: the class rx thread's control transfer against the hub releasing the port; fixed in the vendored rndis / cdc_ncm / cdc_ecm / asix drivers, `cherryusb/PROVENANCE.md`, the 2026-10-12 entry). The DUT at home in a USB mode, `usb_host_manager.prefer_usb_route` set for the run so the default route is the wire, a data destination row to a receiver on betty (one POST every 5 s over LTE; the Pi gets a host route to betty); per cycle the wire gone and back within 150 s, `vbus_cycles` grown, the receiver's count grown, `boot_count` and `unexpected_resets` unchanged; stops at the first reset (three quick crashes park the device) and prints the crash note; each cycle waits for the dongle's LTE to re-attach (`dongle.stage connected`, 30 to 100 s after a VBUS cycle) before it expects the POSTs again; everything put back.

**2026-10-12 16:09, run 1 on the fixed image (ELF 39b4283d93d8ae90): 8 of 8 repairs without a reset** (the wire gone in 1 s and back in 8 to 10 s every time, `vbus_cycles` 0 -> 8, `unexpected_resets` unchanged, 0 own E lines, heap floor 45 KB; the same POST on the previous image crashed at the first try in the garage bench). That run's traffic checks failed on the bench itself (20 s spacing: the dongle rebooted before its LTE was back, so the POSTs pinned to the wire had no way out; the LTE wait above is the fix) and its receiver start had the `pkill -f` self-match in a second form (the file name in the same command line; the kill is its own command now). Run 2 (16:29, the timing fix in): 4 of 4 repairs without a reset again (12 of 12 on the image), but betty stopped answering ssh from the PC during the run (it pings, TCP 22 filtered: the look of a rate ban after two hours of bench ssh sessions), so every betty-dependent check failed on the rig; `--server none` runs the repairs and the reset checks alone.

**Run 3 (17:07, `--server none --n 6`): `ESPNL REPAIR HAMMER PASS`**, 6 of 6 (the wire gone in 1 to 4 s, back in 8 to 10 s, cycle max 14.9 s), 18 of 18 repairs on the image without a reset over the three runs, 0 own E lines, heap floor 45.6 KB. The benches pool their ssh since that afternoon (`tools/testbench/lib/ssh_channel.py`: one session per host per run).
