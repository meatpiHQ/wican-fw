# OBD chip soak: a field configuration, accelerated, until the chip fails

Command: `python tools\testbench\obd\obd_chip_soak_bench.py [--dut localhost:18081] [--sim http://192.168.8.1] [--psu auto] [--console auto|none] [--config "internal/wican config_2026-10-28.json"] [--out DIR] [--stop-file DIR/STOP] [--rounds N] [--only a,b] [--period-ms 50] [--setup-only] [--no-restore]` (PC, the DUT through an ssh tunnel `ssh -N -L 18081:<dut>:80 rpi001`, the PSU on its FTDI port, the console tailed on the CH344 B channel)

Needs: main fw on DUT + rpi001 + the ECU simulator on the bus at 500 kbit/s + the `/ws/obd` bridge (ships enabled) + the PSU. The user's configuration file under `internal/` (gitignored). Runs until the chip fails, the stop file appears or Ctrl-C.

The index row and the other stages: [../TESTING.md](../TESTING.md). The chip's history: `internal/TASK_obd_chip_dead_after_nap.md`, `internal/OBD_CHIP_COMMANDS_V6_VS_V4.md`; the related stages [attpstore.md](attpstore.md), [obdbringup.md](obdbringup.md), [obdfw.md](obdfw.md), [sleepmatrix.md](sleepmatrix.md).

## What it covers

Issue #1023's second report: a WiCAN PRO whose OBD chip stopped answering after a while in an Audi Q4 e-tron on the "Audi: EV MEB" profile (the chip died inside a nap; the mechanism was never identified, see `obd-chip-dead-after-nap`). Ali's ask (2026-11-04): put the bench unit on that user's exact configuration (`internal/wican config_2026-10-28.json`, the legacy export: the car profile with its 14 rows, sleep on at 13.1 V after 5 min, the periodic wake on, requests paused under the sleep voltage, standard PIDs off), poll the profile at the scheduler's floor instead of the user's 5 s so a day of polling takes an hour, play everything a day in the car does to the chip (sleep, periodic wakes, power cuts, cranking dips, restarts, an app), and keep going until the chip fails or he stops it: the proof that the current firmware does not break the chip.

The user's file is the legacy v4 export; v6 has no importer for it, so the bench translates it: the profile rows become `specific` rows (`cmd`, `init` = the row's `pid_init`, the expressions, units, classes and bounds as published; the web UI's import does the same), the car's `init` (`ATAT1;ATST96;`) becomes the store entry's `specific_init` with the profile name attached, `standard_pids disable` = `std_enabled false`, `disable_on_sleep_voltage automate_threshold` = `pause_follow_sleep true` (requests only), `sleep_volt 13.1` = `sleep_mv 13100` with v6's 13.2 V wake, the delay at the schema's floor (1 min, the user's 5), the periodic wake at the floor (5 min, the user's 60). The MQTT, webhook and WiFi parts of the file are not applied (the bench unit's own network stays).

The simulator plays the car: `tools/testbench/obd/fixtures/ecu_sim_meb_soak_profile.json` = the simulator's default profile (VIN `1WCAN0FW0P0000001`, the 16 standard PIDs, the bus) with the MEB profile's 12 DIDs converted by the simulator's own converter (`wican_convert.js --convert`), each on the id the profile addresses: the BMS at 29-bit `17FC007B` / `17FE007B`, the charger at `17FC0076`, the gateway at 11-bit `710` / `77A` and `746` / `7B0`; the simulator makes an extended-id link for a DID whose id is above 0x7FF whatever its bus setting. Every row of the user's file answered through `POST /api/autopid/test` on 2026-11-04 (the two-DID row `221E3B1E3D` as a 29-bit multi-frame too); `HV_A` decodes to nothing because its expression indexes past the reply (`B9` of a 7-byte payload), a defect of the published profile that costs one failed poll per cycle, as on the real car.

The scenarios, in a fixed order per round (`--only` picks, `--seed` shuffles):

| Scenario | What is done | What must hold |
|---|---|---|
| poll | 120 s at 13.5 V | at least 100 answered polls, the simulator answered at least 100 requests, SOC has a value |
| engine_off_sleep | 12.5 V; the supply current proves the entry; 120 s of naps; 13.5 V | requests paused within 12 s, the entry within 160 s, no spurious wake, no HTTP mid-soak, the wake record `power_wake` and nothing else, the chip up, polling back |
| periodic_checkin | 12.5 V across one 5 min periodic wake | the check-in reboot within 7 min of the entry, HTTP and the chip up at 12.5 V, the re-entry within 3 min, records `periodic_wake` then `power_wake`, never `internal_recovery` |
| power_cut | the supply off 5 s | the cold boot comes back, the chip up (awake at power-on), polling back |
| crank_dip | 9.0 V for 2 s | whether or not the ESP resets in the dip (counted), the chip up and polled afterwards |
| sw_restart | `POST /api/restart` | the record `user_request`, the chip up on the warm path, polling back |
| app_session | over `/ws/obd`: `ATZ`, `ATE0`, `ATH1`, `ATSP6`, `0100`, `010C` x5, `ATZ` | the banner, `41 00`, `41 0C`; AutoPID yields and resumes within 14 s; `app_resets` +2 |
| store_read | `ATZ` + `ATE0` + `ATDPN` over `/ws/obd` | the chip's stored protocol as found (the profile's seven flips per cycle wrote it on beta.5) |
| fast_hysteresis | 12.5 V for 20 s and back, three times | paused each time, running again each time, no sleep |

After every scenario `health()`: the chip `ready` (90 s after a boot, 10 s otherwise), the log ring's new E lines, the restart history (new records, `unexpected_resets`), the faults, and at 13.5 V the polling counters moving (`polls_ok` +5 in 30 s).

**The stop rule (verdict FAIL, the DUT left untouched for the post-mortem, the supply kept on):** the chip not ready in time, a `chip not responding` / `bring-up failed` / `chip unresponsive` / `not ready for too long` / `device degraded` E line, no answered poll in 60 s at 13.5 V while the poller runs and the chip claims ready (the report then asked directly: `answered` or `silent` in the verdict), a `will not sleep; recovery reboot`, the chip not up at a periodic check-in. The post-mortem (`chip_failure.json`, `ring_at_failure.txt`, `console.log` in the out folder) holds `/api/obd_chip`, the report, a 13-baud probe scan, the history, the faults, the supply reading.

**A finding (logged, counted, the run goes on):** a crash note, an unexpected reset the scenario did not cause, any other E line, a fault, a scenario assertion (no sleep entry, a missing wake record, the store moved, the app not answered). The round summary and `state.json` carry every finding; the verdict says how many.

**Outputs** under `--out` (default `test-reports/logs/obd_chip_soak_<stamp>/`): `soak.log` (every line the bench printed, stamped), `state.json` (per-scenario pass and finding counts, the poll rates, the store reads, the findings), `as_found.json` (what the restore puts back), `console.log` (the DUT's console beside the run, with the restart tracker's lines and any panic), the post-mortem files on a failure.

**Restore** (`finally`, skipped with `--no-restore` and on a chip failure): the vehicle entry's profile and init, `config.json`, the `autopid` and `sleep_manager` settings as found (one planned restart), the simulator back on its found profile with the bench profile deleted, the supply back at its found setpoint.

## Traps

- **"The chip's protocol store moved 6 -> 7" (run 2 round 7 at 05:46, run 3 twice) was the bench's read, not a write (RESOLVED 06:10):** the read sent `ATZ` straight at a chip busy with a poll; the chip answers `STOPPED` and DISCARDS the word (the ELM327 rule, the same thing the app leg met), so the `ATDPN` that followed reported the CURRENT protocol of the row in flight (7 for a BMS row, 6 for a gateway row), not the store. Every read taken with a CR first, or with AutoPID off, said 6, and a warm start does reload the stored protocol (set 7 in RAM, `ATWS`, it reads 6: proven 05:57). The hand replay of the poller's exact 29-bit row (`ATM0`, `ATTP7`, `ATCP17`, `ATSHFC007B`, `ATFCSH17FC007B`, `ATCRA17FE007B`, `22028C` answered) left the store at 6 across a power cycle; the control with the memory function ON (the probe route's unguarded `ATM1`, then the same row without `ATM0`) wrote 7 and it survived a power cycle: a real EEPROM write, which the driver's `ATM0` ahead of every switch prevents (`memory_off` steps once per polled row: 421 per 420 polls). The read sends a CR first and verifies the banner since then (`?` when there is none, never a value). Scripts: the session scratchpad's `store_write_probe.py` (read / memon / seq). The store was put back to 6 (one write each way: two EEPROM cycles on the bench chip).
- **A stale `console.log.stop` ends the console tail at once:** the second run (02:37) had 0 console lines because the first run's stop file was still in the folder. The bench removes it before starting the tail since then; check `console.log.keys` for `console_tail: N lines` at the start of a run.
- **An app's first command lands on a busy chip (run 1, 02:33, a product observation for Ali, not a chip failure):** with the profile polled at the floor a request is always in flight, so the app's first bytes make the chip abort it with `STOPPED >` and the chip DISCARDS the app's command (the ELM327 rule). The bench's `ATZ` (sent by the driver as `ATWS`) was lost that way: the session went on with the chip's echo off and, more to the point, on the headers the profile's last row had left (`ATSH710`, `ATCRA77A`): the app's `0100` went to 710 and was answered from 77A instead of 7DF/7E8. On a real car an app that does not set its own headers after connecting would read nothing until it does. Real apps send a CR first and wait for the prompt; the bench does the same since that run and counts the `STOPPED` (`app_first_stopped` in state.json). Whether the driver should hold an app's bytes until the in-flight AutoPID request has ended is Ali's call.
- The bench unit was replaced on 2026-11-04 (Ali: a fresh unit from the latest batch, 44bd8d73ca79, MAC 44:BD:8D:73:CA:78, console still COM254, its USB-C on the PC as the CH342 pair). The unit's USB-C on the PC does NOT show up as a network adapter on this firmware (the "WiCAN Pro" NCM adapter on the PC is the simulator), so the AP is the way in. `test.ps1` needs `-DeviceId 44bd8d73ca79` to resolve it; its default still names the old unit.

- A fresh unit has no car in its store: the bench runs the detection job (`POST /api/autopid/vehicles/detect`) against the simulator first and attaches the profile to the car it finds.

The bench uploads its own `meb_soak.json`, reloads it warm (the bus stays up; the simulator's `id_format` setting stays `11bit`, the 29-bit DIDs get their own links), and deletes it at the end.
