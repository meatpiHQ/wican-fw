# The OBD chip's firmware update, guarded

Command: `.\test.ps1 chipguard` (= `python tools\testbench\obd\chip_guard_bench.py --dut localhost:18081 [--fw22 <V2.3.22.txt>] [--off-s 60] [--psu auto]`; the stage opens the tunnel through rpi001).

Needs: the DUT on the bench AP, rpi001 (the tunnel), the simulator's supply (`tools/testbench/lib/bench_psu.py` `hold_off()`: the bench takes the DUT's power away for a minute; the simulator firmware of 2026-11-21 or later holds a plain power-off, an older one is driven through its setting), the components tree for the V2.3.22 image (`MEATPI_COMPONENTS_PATH`, else the dev layout). Prints `CHIP GUARD PASS`, 23 checks with AutoPID polling (21 without; two fewer on a unit found on V2.3.22), about 3 minutes, one restart asked for in the middle of the first write, one power cycle, two chip writes.

The index row and the other stages: [../TESTING.md](../TESTING.md). The update's own paths (no forced rewrite, the file, the interrupted stream): [obdfw.md](obdfw.md). The pages: [chip_prompt_bench.md](chip_prompt_bench.md), [probe_chip_prompt.md](probe_chip_prompt.md).

## What it covers

GitHub issue #1075 (2026-11-21): a chip that never came up after an update from 6.0.0-beta.5 to 6.0.0-beta.12. Not reproduced in 13 passes on the bench (the record: `internal/issue_1075/README.md`). Ali's answer to "what is the safe way" is a sequence the device enforces around the user's update of the chip:

- the update is accepted only in a start that began with the power coming on (the page tells the user to unplug the adapter, wait one minute and plug it back in);
- AutoPID and every other task stay off the chip from the update's first step until the chip is up again;
- a warm start and a closed protocol (`ATWS`, `ATPC`) are the last words the chip's firmware hears before it is written;
- no sleep meanwhile;
- no restart meanwhile (2026-11-21, evening, Ali: "Device blocks it"): `POST /api/restart` answers 409 with the reason, and a restart the firmware plans itself waits until the chip is up again.

The bench puts the chip on V2.3.22 (the V2.3.22 image as `/data/V2.3.99.txt`, the firmware's "the name is the version" contract, with the bench's `any_start` word) and walks the user's way:

| Check | What is asserted |
|---|---|
| G1 | the V2.3.22 image is taken with `any_start`; the console's `system -r`, typed 6 s into that write, restarts the unit only when every record is written (the unit stays up, the last status read shows the last record); behind the restart the chip is on V2.3.22, ready and flagged outdated (a write cut short would have left the chip in its download mode, and that start would have put the PACKAGED image on it) |
| G2 | behind a planned restart `fw.power_on` is false; `POST /api/obd_chip/update {}` is 409 with `power_cycle` and the way out in words; no job ran, the chip is untouched |
| G3 | the supply off for `--off-s` seconds (60) and on again: the start's record is a power-on reset, not planned; `fw.power_on` is true, the chip ready on V2.3.22, `reset.pulses` 0 |
| G4 | `POST {}` is 202; a second one meanwhile 409 (already running); a test request (`POST /api/autopid/test`) and a wire probe in the middle of the write are refused; `POST /api/restart` in the middle of the write is 409 with the reason and the unit does not restart (its boot count stands); the job ends `ESP_OK` with every record; the chip is back on the packaged version, ready, not outdated, inside 60 s; AutoPID reports `paused_chip_fw` from inside 3 s of the request for the whole job, `polls_ok` moves by 2 at most meanwhile and `polls_failed` not at all |
| G5 | AutoPID polls again and nothing fails; the log ring in order: `paused: the OBD chip's firmware is being updated`, `chip warm-started and its protocol closed: writing`, `starting chip firmware update from builtin`, `update job done: bringing the chip up again`, `bring-up done in`, `resumed: the OBD chip's firmware update ended`; no reset pulse, no fault code; no E line and no unexpected reset since the power came on |

The counter checks need AutoPID polling a car (the bench unit polls the simulator's MEB profile); on a unit that does not poll they are skipped and the bench says so.

## Records

With AutoPID polling a profile at its shortest period, one of four update requests did not start: the console read `builtin V2.3.24: not run, the chip stayed claimed for 5 s (ESP_ERR_TIMEOUT)`, nothing was written and the page would have said "the update did not start" (a claim is polled, not queued, and AutoPID's requests followed each other without a gap the update's 10 ms looks could find). ## Traps

- **Why a power cut needed work on the simulator (2026-11-21).** The DUT's own two COM ports leave this PC 40 to 65 ms after its power is cut. A program on the PC (found: a mouse and keyboard utility running in the background; with it closed nothing resets) then opens the COM ports it still sees, the simulator's debug port among them, with DTR and RTS asserted, and closes it: that sequence resets the simulator's chip (reason `usb`), and until its firmware of 2026-11-21 the boot behind the reset switched the output on again, so a cut by `output off` lasted 4 to 12 s. Now and then the same sequence leaves the simulator in its ROM download mode, dead until it is reset. Since that firmware a port switched off by command stays off across such a reset (`held_off` on the simulator's `/api/power`), and `SimPsu.hold_off(seconds)` is output off, wait, output on; opening a supply releases the debug port's lines (a stray open then resets nothing) and a simulator found in its ROM is fetched back with esptool, with a WARNING line. Against an older simulator firmware `hold_off` goes through the setting `obd_power.output_on_boot` (false + submit, wait, true + submit): if the bench dies between the two, set it true on the simulator by hand and submit. The record: the simulator repo's `docs/S31_PORT_STATUS.md` and its `tools/testbench/power_hold_bench.py`.
- **The serial flash's reset reads as power-on.** `build.ps1 -Flash` ends with a reset by the EN pin, which the ESP32-S3 reports as a power-on reset: `fw.power_on` is true behind a flash although the OBD chip never lost power. The bench restarts the unit by request first (G2), so it does not depend on what the run before left.
- **Only one stage on the tunnel's port.** The stage's tunnel is `localhost:18081`, as for `uploadconfirm` and `fwupdate`: a tunnel left open by hand makes the stage stop with "port 18081 busy".
- The chip is written twice. A unit found on V2.3.22 is not written down and ends on the packaged version: the bench never downgrades a chip for good.
- The car comes from the simulator too. Should the simulator restart at the cut (its firmware from before 2026-11-21 did, every time), the car is away for a few seconds: the bench waits for AutoPID to run again before it takes its counters.
