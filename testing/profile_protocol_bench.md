# A profile with ATSP6 and ATSP7 rows beside the chip's one protocol save

Command: `python tools\testbench\obd\profile_protocol_bench.py [--dut localhost:18081] [--write] [--only flip,pinned,swap,byprofile,write]` (PC, the DUT through an ssh tunnel `ssh -N -L 18081:<dut>:80 rpi001`, ECU sim v2 at its own address)

Needs: main fw on DUT + rpi001 + ECU sim v2 on the bus at 500 kbit/s + the `/ws/obd` bridge, the chip's store reading `6`. Prints `PROFILE PROTOCOL PASS`. Without `--write`: no write of the chip, six planned restarts of the DUT, about 7 min. With it: TWO writes of the chip's protocol store (the flip and the way back), four more restarts, about 10 min.

The index row and the other stages: [../TESTING.md](../TESTING.md). The siblings: [protocol_save_bench.md](protocol_save_bench.md) (the save itself), [attpstore.md](attpstore.md) (the switches that must not write), [obdchipsoak.md](obdchipsoak.md) (the same profile for hours), [autopid_init_leak_bench.md](autopid_init_leak_bench.md) (what a row leaves behind for the next row type).

## What it covers

The firmware saves ONE protocol per car in the OBD chip (a real `ATSP` after the detection job). Most vehicle profiles pin a protocol themselves, and some pin both: the Audi / VW MEB profile has `ATSP7` rows for the battery ECU beside `ATSP6` rows for the gateway. Ali, 2026-11-10: "Keep the one protocol save per car, but we need to make sure it does not affect cars with ATSP6 and ATSP7 in there car profiles". Four things have to hold, and this bench reads each of them from the device:

1. the rows of such a profile poll right whatever protocol the chip holds;
2. polling them never writes the chip (every `ATSP` of a row goes out as `ATTP` behind an acknowledged `ATM0`);
3. the profile's protocol never becomes what is saved: the save takes only a protocol a standard request answered on;
4. the detection job, which decides what is saved, works while such rows are being polled;
5. first contact finds the car by itself when the only rows that answer pin another protocol than the car's own;
6. the Quick Setup's probe confirms a car by a profile request whose reply carries a 29-bit id.

And one thing the bench met on the way, which is about any table and not about these profiles: tables put in the place of other tables send their own inits.

None but the first two held until 2026-11-10. The first two findings were fixed that afternoon, the other three that evening.

- **First contact asked on a row's protocol.** With no protocol in effect (no car current, or a stored protocol that stayed silent at boot), the identity check takes the chip's current protocol (`ATDPN`) for what the chip's search found. When the poll that just answered was a row with its own `ATSP7`, the chip said 7 because the row had put it there. The identity requests went out on 7, the car's standard requests live on 6: `vehicle identity: vin (none), 0 ECUs, uds (protocol re-detected)` three times, and no identity for the rest of that boot. Such a poll now starts a walk with standard requests, on the row's protocol first and then on the others of the bus (`contact_behind_row()`; the rule is `ap_veh_dpn_is_search()`, host-tested).
- **The probe passed over a 29-bit reply.** The reply parser takes four leading bytes for a 29-bit id only when they are the legislated `18 DA F1 xx`, or when it was told the row's own receive id or that the protocol in effect is 29-bit. The poller and test-a-PID tell it before every reply; the probe did not and parsed with whatever the row polled last had left. Proof on one image with one detect body (the two `ATSP7` requests only, 2026-11-10 21:54): behind a polled `ATSP6` row the job failed with "no ECU response (ignition on?)", behind a polled `ATSP7` row it confirmed the car by `22028C`. In the Quick Setup, where standard rows are what is polled, the car was confirmed by the profile's third request (the `ATSP6` one) or not at all. The probe now sets the hint itself (`ap_chain_reply_hint()`, host-tested).
- **New tables kept the old row's init.** The runner knows "the row I sent last" by its index, and a reload of the tables did not reset that. The row that landed on the index polled last went out without its own init. Several rows healed at the next row; ONE row never did: `222AB6` (the gateway, `ATSP6`) in the place of `22028C` (the battery ECU, `ATSP7`) answered 0 polls of 12 and had no value until a restart. The poller now forgets what it sent when it picks its first row from new tables.
- **The profile's protocol was saved.** A car that answers no standard request is confirmed by the Quick Setup's probe with the profile's own requests, and the wizard names the protocol of the profile's first `ATSP`. The detection job handed that protocol to the save. On the bench the probe named 7, the request that answered was a row pinned on 6, and `ATSP7` was written to a chip that held 6. `ap_veh_chip_learns()` in the components repo's autopid decides now (host test `test_veh_chip_learns_only_a_standard_answer`).
- **The detection job ran on top of what a row had left in the chip.** A profile row's own flow control (`ATFCSH17FC007B;ATFCSD300000;ATFCSM1`) stayed in effect under the job and under the identity check: on the wire `0902` came back as its first frame only (`7E8 10 14 49 02 01 31 57 43`) until an `ATFCSM0`. With only profile rows in the tables the job failed with "no ECU response (ignition on?)", 3 of 3. Every prelude of the firmware's own carries the undo of what the tables moved now, and the job asks for it before its first request (`send_prelude_clean()`, `ap_runner_undo_tables()` in `autopid_baseline.c`).

The simulator plays a car of the bench's own (the soak's fixture with the VIN `1WCAN0FW0P0000003`, uploaded as `profile_proto.json` and made the simulator's active profile, so it survives the simulator's reboots). The DUT gets six rows for it: `EngineRPM` and `VehicleSpeed` (standard) and four rows of the published MEB profile, `SOC` and `BATT_TEMP` on `ATSP7`, `RANGE` and `OUTSIDE_TEMPERATURE` on `ATSP6`, in an order that changes the protocol at every row. The unit's own car is never touched.

## Traps

- **Forgetting the current car leaves its rows live and no car current.** A boot in that state polls rows that pin a protocol with nothing naming the car's own. Until 2026-11-10 first contact then took the row's protocol for the one the chip found; the pinned leg stages exactly this. The other legs make the unit's own car current before every restart that is to meet an unknown car.
- **A table of one row sends its init once.** The switch and its `ATM0` go out with the first poll, not with every poll: the window's "one `ATM0` per poll" holds only for tables whose rows alternate (`window(..., per_poll=False)` for the swap leg).
- **The detection's two time stamps differ.** `ts` on `GET /api/autopid/std_scan` is taken when the job ends, the one in `/std_scan/result` when the document is built, a store write earlier: the bench takes a result for the run's own when the two are within 5 s (`scan_result()`).
- **The simulator's fault injection is cleared by `DELETE /api/ecu/errors/<mode>/<pid>` (the entry stays listed with `enabled` false) and by its reboot.** The bench deletes both entries whenever it puts the car on a bus.
- **The simulator answers a profile's own requests only on a bus that has an OBD link.** With `hs_obd11` and `hs_obd29` both off it answers nothing at all, the profile's requests included; a car that is silent to standard requests is staged with the fault injection on `0100` and `0902` instead.
- **The release stage wants port 18081 for its own tunnel.** A tunnel left open by a bench makes `.\test.ps1 release` fail in 8 s with "no tunnel to the DUT through rpi001 (port 18081 busy?)".
- A run cut short between the flip and the way back leaves the store on the other protocol: the restore's own way back runs when the store does not read as found, and its last check says so when that failed.

## Open finding (2026-11-10, read in the code, not reproduced, not fixed)

- **The Quick Setup's probe sends the profile's protocol without asking the bus guard.** A row of the tables asks `ap_guard_chain_ok()` for every protocol its chains set before anything of it is sent, so a row pinned on a 500 kbit/s protocol stays silent on a 250 kbit/s bus. The probe (`ap_scan_probe_run()`) sends the prelude of the profile's protocol and the requests' own inits as they are. A car profile chosen in the Quick Setup on a vehicle whose bus runs at another bit rate and answers nothing standard (a J1939 truck) would put frames at the wrong bit rate on that bus. To reproduce: the simulator's bus at 250 kbit/s with traffic, a detect body with a profile pinned on 6, the error frames counted on the PCAN.

The two findings this section listed until the evening of 2026-11-10 (the identity check on a row's protocol, the probe and 29-bit replies) are the pinned leg and the byprofile leg's request check now.
