# can.send: a raw frame on any bus of the OBD connector

Command: `.\test.ps1 cansend [-SkipPrecan]` (PC; it opens the tunnel and runs `python tools\testbench\obd\can_send_bench.py [--dut localhost:18081] [--skip-precan] [--precan vpw,iso9141] [--only route,buses,can,seq,noack,wake,script,app,precan,polling]`)

Needs: main fw on DUT + rpi001 + ECU sim v2 with every bus wired to the DUT's OBD connector (its four CAN interfaces, the K-line, J1850) and as the DUT's supply. Prints `CAN SEND PASS`. About 5 min, up to six planned restarts of the DUT (Automate off and the script engine on, the protocol pinned for each car before CAN, the protocol back, Automate on with a rule, the settings as found), three with `-SkipPrecan`, three restarts of the simulator, no write of the OBD chip. The unit's settings and tables and the simulator are put back as found; Automate may be on or off at the start.

The index row and the other stages: [../TESTING.md](../TESTING.md). The feature: the `autopid` README in the components repo, "can.send", and `components/HTTP_API.md` (`POST /api/can/send`). The basis: [chip_can_slot_bench.md](chip_can_slot_bench.md) (a bus through a definition in the chip's RAM), [autopid_bus_bench.md](autopid_bus_bench.md) (rows on those buses). The host half: `.\test.ps1 host autopid` (`test_send.c`, 8 cases, [host.md](host.md)). The page: `node tools\webui_preview\probe_rules.mjs` (`RULES PROBE PASS`, the action "Send a CAN frame").

## What it covers

`POST /api/can/send`, the rule action `can.send` and the script binding `can_send()` put raw frames on a bus by name (GitHub issue #1072): `can` is WiCAN's own CAN controller, `obd` the car's OBD protocol through the OBD chip, `ms`, `sw` and `ch` MS, single-wire and CH CAN through the chip, and `wake` sends a single-wire frame in the transceiver's high-voltage wake mode. The witness of what was on a wire is ECU sim v2's receive log (`GET /api/ecu/rxlog`: for every frame it read, the bus, the id and its length, the data length and the bytes), so a check does not ask "did the unit say sent" but "did the other end read this frame, on this bus, as given".

## Traps

- **ECU sim v2's receive log leaves out its newest frames when its ring is full.** The ring holds 64 frames and a read lists the oldest 60 (its JSON buffer ends there), so on a quiet bus a frame just sent does not show until four more arrive. The first run of this bench read "nothing on the bus" for every frame the chip had sent and the simulator had acknowledged. The bench clears the log (`DELETE /api/ecu/rxlog`) before every frame it expects; a busy bus hides the gap (the frame shows a second later).
- **The wake leg needs the simulator's single-wire controller FRESH.** Its error state is the witness, and it only goes one way without traffic: the bench resets the simulator's co-processor (`POST /api/c5/reset`) before each turn and waits for `active`. The state arrives about a second late, and for a moment behind the reset the status carries none; the bench waits three seconds before it reads "untouched".
- **Behind that reset the simulator acknowledges nothing on single-wire and CH CAN for about a second.** The issue's sequence is sent after a probe frame was acknowledged.
- **A stage through `test.ps1` opens its own tunnel on 18081**: close one of your own first.
- The leg `can` needs the unit's own CAN controller enabled and on the bus (the bench unit's canonical set). On a unit with it off the leg checks the refusal and says so.
- The script leg switches the script engine on for the run (a planned restart, shared with Automate off) and the last restart puts it back.
- A send through the chip from the bench right behind `chip_nvm` (an app on `/ws/obd`) is refused for ten seconds: the bench waits them out.
