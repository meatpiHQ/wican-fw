# Automate rows on MS, single-wire and CH CAN beside the car's OBD protocol

Command: `.\test.ps1 multibus` (PC; it opens the tunnel and runs `python tools\testbench\obd\autopid_bus_bench.py [--dut localhost:18081] [--only test,speed,store,filter,hold,poll,guard,park,pause,app,raw,listen]`)

Needs: main fw on DUT + rpi001 + ECU sim v2 with its four CAN buses wired to the DUT's OBD connector and as the DUT's supply, Automate off at the start, a voltage pause in effect under the supply (the default: it follows the sleep voltage). Prints `AUTOPID BUS PASS`. About 9 min, five planned restarts of the DUT (two for the stored filter, Automate on with a held init, the init as found, Automate back), nine restarts of the simulator, no write of the OBD chip. The unit's tables, its Automate settings, the supply and the simulator are put back as found.

The index row and the other stages: [../TESTING.md](../TESTING.md). The basis: [chip_can_slot_bench.md](chip_can_slot_bench.md) (the chip reaches these buses through a definition in its RAM), [chip_word_class_bench.md](chip_word_class_bench.md) (a preset is stored by the chip at every selection). The host half: `.\test.ps1 host autopid` (`test_bus.c`, 11 cases, and `test_store_words.c`, 5 cases, [host.md](host.md)). The page: `node tools\webui_preview\probe_row_bus.mjs` (`ROW BUS PROBE PASS`, 52 checks over the mock). A filter's Bus field: `node tools\webui_preview\probe_filter_bus.mjs` (`FILTER BUS PROBE PASS`, 28 checks).

## What it covers

A row of the Automate tables has a bus: the car's OBD protocol (the default), MS CAN, single-wire CAN or CH CAN, and a speed for a car whose bus does not run at the usual one. The firmware asks the rows of a bus together, takes the OBD chip there through an AT user protocol defined in the chip's RAM, and brings it home as soon as no row of another bus is due. The simulator's four CAN interfaces answer the same OBD requests, so "answered on that bus" is checked by the value (800 rpm) and, where it matters, by the bus being the only one at that speed.

## Traps

- **A request with a reply count (`010C1`) keeps a round short.** Without it the chip waits out its timeout (`ATST96`: about 600 ms) on a functional request, on every bus; seven such rows are a 4 s round.
- **The Test route answers 200 with `ok` true and `raw` `CAN ERROR`** when nobody acknowledges: the exchange with the chip worked. Judge by the value or the reply bytes.
- **`CAN ERROR` while the simulator restarts counts too**: the park leg's three replies came during the restart that changed the simulator's speed.
- **A row backs off after three failures** (its period times four): after the park the bench waits four periods before it looks for answers again. The same holds for the rows of the OTHER buses behind a restart of the simulator (nobody answers while it restarts): the park leg waits one backed-off period before it counts their answers. Without it the single-wire row was met once in three rounds (15:34, the red run).
- **The pause leg lowers the unit's supply** (ECU sim v2 is the supply) to 0.6 V under the pause's threshold for about half a minute and puts it back in its `finally`. The unit must not go to sleep in that time: the leg refuses to start when sleep is on with a delay under two minutes. A restart of the simulator puts the supply back to its boot default, so the leg restarts nothing.
- **The simulator's `requests` count is one number for all its buses**: the pause leg first checks that it moves while polling, then that it stands still.
- **ECU sim v2's single-wire transceiver sleeps by default** and goes back to sleep at every restart of the simulator: the bench wakes it after each one.
- **A row that is refused does not stay refused in every table.** Three failures in a row and its period is times four; it is then asked at another moment of the round, and when that moment is right behind a restore of the baseline (the chip's return from another bus, a change of row type) a reading that depends on what the rows before it left behind comes out right, and stays right. "Has a value" and the bus's counters over a late window pass such a row. Count each row's publications (`ts_us` of its value, one look every 0.3 s), count failures from the moment the tables go in, and test rows of one type on one bus by themselves (the leg `raw`, 2026-11-23).
- **ECU sim v2 has no GMLAN ECU.** It answers service `AA` with the refusal `03 7F AA 11`; the leg `raw` uses that frame as "an answer with no service to compare" and reads its byte 3. A real `$AA` answer comes on another id (`54C` on the reporter's car) with the packet number first.

- **ECU sim v2's single-wire controller goes bus-off when its broadcast starts on a sleeping transceiver, and it does not come back (2026-11-26).** The transceiver sleeps at the simulator's start (`tester_io.swcan_mode`, default `sleep`) and a broadcast that the settings switch on starts at once. From then on `/api/ecu/status` reads `sw_can.bridge_state` `bus-off`: with nothing left to send, with the transceiver woken through `/api/tester`, with its load on, with the chip acknowledging. A filter's window on single-wire catches nothing and a request there ends in `CAN ERROR`. The leg `listen` sets `swcan_mode` `normal` in the same restart as the broadcast and puts both back at its end; it asks first that no controller reads `bus-off`. CH CAN, on the same bridge, stays `passive` and needs nothing. The first probes of pull request 15 took this for "single-wire cannot be tested on the bench".
- **One ID of the simulator's broadcast is on the wire of a bus at a time.** The chip monitors without acknowledging (`ATCSM1`, its default), and the simulator is the only other node on MS, CH and single-wire CAN. An unacknowledged frame is repeated for ever, so the first frame of the simulator's queue is the only one a listener sees (MS: `tx_frames` stands, `bus_errors` climb by about 1000 a second; CH and single-wire: `passive`). A filter of another ID of the broadcast catches nothing. To change the ID: switch the frames (`POST /api/ecu/broadcast`), then a request of a row on each bus, during which the chip is a normal node and the queue moves on (`sim_queue_on()`).
- **The simulator's `tx_frames` on CH and single-wire CAN count what was handed to its bridge,** acknowledged or not; on MS CAN they are the controller's own count of frames that went through. `bridge_state` is the one to read there.
- **An app's own `ATMA` on a bus with an unacknowledged frame floods** (MS CAN: 54,000 lines for 3 s of listening, a minute to drain). The firmware's window ends at the first frame; a probe by hand should set a receive filter for an ID nobody sends, or use the Test route.
