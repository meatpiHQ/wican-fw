# A pre-CAN car found without being asked for, and by the detection

Command: `.\test.ps1 precan [-Detect]` (PC; it opens the tunnel and runs `python tools\testbench\obd\precan_contact_bench.py [--dut localhost:18081] [--only test,body,startup] [--detect] [--can off|on]`; with `--only` given, the detect leg of a car never seen runs only when `detect` is among the words)

Needs: main fw on DUT + rpi001 + ECU sim v2 (firmware v1.00-16 or later for `-Detect`) with its K-line and J1850 buses wired to the DUT's OBD connector, Automate off at the start, the unit's own car a CAN one with a VIN; for the leg `body` the PCAN adapter with a channel on the DUT's bus (the leg finds the channel by listening; without one it says so and runs on the simulator's repeated frame). Prints `PRECAN CONTACT PASS`. About 9 min, eight planned restarts of the DUT, about fifteen restarts of the simulator, no write of the OBD chip. With `-Detect`: about 3 min more, six more restarts, and five writes of the chip's stored protocol (the one save a detection makes by design: to 2, to 5 and back, and to 5 and back for the car behind a body bus; 12 min and fourteen restarts on 2026-11-27). The unit's tables, its Automate settings, the vehicle store (the car's name, profile and init with it) and the simulator are put back as found.

The index row and the other stages: [../TESTING.md](../TESTING.md). The host half: `.\test.ps1 host autopid` (`test_dialect_precan_candidates`, the walk's order in `test_precan_protocol_table`), [host.md](host.md). The neighbour stage: [kline_accept_bench.md](kline_accept_bench.md) (the Test call, the poll, the trouble codes on these protocols).

## What it covers

A unit plugged into a K-line or SAE J1850 car polled the protocol of the car it knew, gave it up for the chip's own search and never found the car. The walk that looks for whoever is there ended behind the CAN protocols, and the detection did not try SAE J1850. Now the walk goes on to SAE J1850 and the K-line (`2`, `1`, `5`, `3`, `4`, the cheapest first), in the detection always and at first contact when nobody else talks on the CAN pins. Since 2026-11-27 "always" holds for the detection on a car whose CAN pins carry a bus that cannot be read as well (the leg `body`, GitHub issue #1022); first contact stays parked there.

ECU sim v2 plays one car at a time. Its ECUs all carry the VIN of the bench unit's own car, so first contact meets "the same car on another protocol": the store records the protocol and nothing is written to the OBD chip. A car the unit has never seen is made by forgetting the unit's own car first (`-Detect`).

## Traps

- **The unit's log ring outlives a restart.** A line of the boot before reads like one of this boot. The uptime in front of the lines falls back where a new boot begins.
- **The bench unit's own CAN node is enabled** (the bench's canonical set) and acknowledges the OBD chip's CAN requests. On a car with no CAN the bus guard therefore reports `vehicle bus live at 500 kbit/s`, and a CAN try reads `NO DATA` after the reply wait where a unit with the node off reads `CAN ERROR` at once. Judge "is somebody on the CAN pins" by what is heard while the chip is quiet.
- **The simulator's ECUs carry the same VIN on every bus.** First contact on another of its buses is the unit's own car on another protocol: the record's protocol moves and the chip is not written. The record's ECU set follows too (`10:183F8003` on the K-line and J1850, `7E8:183F8003` on CAN); the stage ends on the CAN car, so it reads the CAN way afterwards.
- **A detection writes the chip's stored protocol, once a boot.** The detect leg restarts between its three detections, or the second save is put off.
- **Forgetting the unit's own car** leaves the store empty until the next contact. A detect leg cut short puts the car back by a start on the CAN car (the stage's last step does it), with one save of the chip's protocol.
- **A unit that polls its CAN car keeps that bus's verdict.** A bus does not change its bit rate under a unit that stays plugged in, so the verdict of an answered poll stands for the boot. Change the simulator's HS CAN to another bit rate under such a unit and its chip asks at 500 kbit/s into it: the first run of the leg `body` counted 228 error frames at the adapter that way, none of them the firmware's fault. The leg switches Automate off before the simulator changes.
- **The body bus needs a second node.** Alone, the simulator repeats its first frame for ever (nobody acknowledges). The leg puts the PCAN adapter on the bus in normal mode at 50 kbit/s; it takes whichever channel reads the frames in a listen-only look, so the adapter's plugs need not be known. Without an adapter on the unit's bus the leg runs on the repeated frame and says so. The first open of a channel at a new bit rate can fail once ("irregularities were registered"); `lib/body_bus.py` opens twice.
- **`kb.sim_groups()` carried `bitrate` and `id_format`**, which are `bench_bus`'s view of ECU sim v2's HS CAN and no keys it stores. As long as no leg left the HS bit rate changed that went unseen; the leg `body` does, and the stage's restore stopped with "ecu_sim has no key ['bitrate']", the simulator left at 50 kbit/s (2026-11-27, corrected in `kline_accept_bench.py`).
- **A car that is forgotten and found again comes back without its profile, its init and a name of its own.** The detect leg does that to the unit's own car, and the stage's store check looks at the key, the name and the protocol: the bench unit's car lost its profile that way. The stage now puts the entry's name, profile and init back and checks them.
- **A round of the walk with nobody there takes about 11 s** (the CAN tries, then 7.9 s of pre-CAN ones), and the gap between two rounds doubles from 10 s to 80 s. A car that turns up late in a boot is found by the next round, up to 80 s later.
