## What it covers

Two sleep-path changes of 2026-11-07 (Ali, after the review of how the chip goes to sleep in v6 against 4.52p):

- **The sleep word.** `obd_chip_sleep(true)` sends `STSLEEP0` (the documented ST sleep command, what 4.x sent at every sleep entry and every re-sleep) and pulls the PowerEn pin behind it. The chip's provisioned state (native control mode, the external, UART and voltage sleep triggers OFF) sleeps on the ST sleep command and on nothing else by the OBDLink and MIC documentation; the pin works through the vendor's `VTPPSW` function alone, so a chip whose PowerEn switch cannot sleep it never slept under v6: six reset pulses, a recovery reboot, the whole sleep delay awake again. The chip answers `OK` with a prompt (`4F 4B 0D 0D 3E`) and sleeps on its own a second or two later, READY following that late, so the `OK` is the verdict (I `chip asleep by command (STSLEEP0 answered OK)`) and the first nap's READY check the ground truth, as before the word; a chip that refuses the word (mid-monitor it answers `?`) or stays silent gets one W line and the pin as before. The nap's re-sleep waits 1.5 s after its reset pulse (the chip's boot) before the word.
- **The PPSW repair.** The bring-up's `VTPPSW10` (a chip whose PowerEn switch is not `10`: the one EEPROM write of this path) is acknowledged, left 600 ms to settle (legacy's 100 + 500 ms) and read back as `10` BEFORE the reset pulse; until 2026-11-07 the pulse came 0 ms after the `OK`. An unreadable value is left alone (no blind write), a refused write gets no pulse.
- **The PPSW digits, learnt by this stage.** `VT PPSW xy` is x = the WAKE trigger, y = the SLEEP trigger (0 low level, 1 high level, 2 rising edge, 3 falling edge, F none): `10` = high wakes, low sleeps; `0F` = a LOW level wakes and nothing sleeps, so a chip on `0F` is woken by our own pin at every entry and re-sleep (the chip's `STSLLT` reads `SLEEP: CMD WAKE: PWR EN`); `FF` is refused (`?`); `2F` = a rising edge wakes, nothing sleeps: the pin low does nothing and the pin static high does nothing, the stand-in for a chip the pin cannot sleep. The vendor's sheet (`internal/MIC_PPSW_POWEREN_INSTRUCTIONS.md`, Ali, 2026-11-07) confirms the digits and adds the bare `VT PPSW` as the way to disable the function altogether, and the 261 s no-sleep protection after a power-on as the pin function's, cancelled by `ATZ` or `ATWS`. A command-slept chip holds its sleep through an ESP reboot and ignores 2 Mbaud words on its UART; the pin's wake level undoes it at once (`10` with the pin high).

## Traps

- The bench owns COM254 for the whole run (it types on it): a console tail beside it cannot open the port, so the stage starts none and the bench writes its own capture per leg into the run's log folder (`console_sleep_word_<leg>_<time>.log`).
- The PPSW stand-in value matters: `0F` wakes the chip on our own pin (a LOW-level WAKE), `FF` is refused, `1F` (a high-level wake) lets the pin high undo a command sleep while the device is awake. `2F` is the one that neither sleeps nor wakes on anything our sequence does; the bench falls back to `1F` and then judges the naps only.
- Two EEPROM writes of the chip's PPSW cell per run (the fault, the repair). Run it on purpose, not in a loop.
- The chip's `STSLLT` and `VTPOWERMANAGE` (both reads, over the probe) say how the chip last slept and woke (`SLEEP: CMD WAKE: PWR EN`); they survive the bring-up's reset pulse and are the fastest way to tell "the pin woke it" from "it never slept".
- The log ring survives the wake reboot, so the entry's lines can also be read from `GET /api/logs/ring` after the wake; the console is the primary source because it also carries the naps.
