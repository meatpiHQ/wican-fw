# A J2534 session when something goes wrong beside it

Command: `.\test.ps1 j2534session` = `python tools\testbench\usb\j2534_session_bench.py --dut localhost:18081 --j2534 localhost:18534 --pi rpi001 --dut-ip <the DUT as the Pi sees it> --pcan <the PCAN channel on the DUT's bus> [--dll <built wican_j2534.dll, 1.10 or later>] [--old-dll <a driver before 1.10>]` (PC; two ssh forwards through rpi001: 18081 to the DUT's port 80, 18534 to its J2534 port 6809; a helper copied to the Pi for the run)

Needs: a firmware with the session fixes of 2026-11-18 on the DUT + rpi001 with `python3` and `sudo -n nft` + PCAN on the DUT's bus (`WICAN_PCAN_DUT`, the adapter's plugs may be swapped) + for leg D the 32-bit Python and a driver DLL from 1.10 (the stage passes `WICAN_J2534_DLL` when set, else `..\..\wican-j2534-driver\build\Release\wican_j2534.dll` when that file exists, and skips the leg with a note when there is none; `WICAN_J2534_OLD_DLL` names a driver before 1.10 for D3). Prints `J2534 SESSION PASS`. About 2.5 min: two planned restarts of the unit when its J2534 server is off as found, 32 s of silence in leg K and about 32 s of waiting in leg V.

The index row and the other stages: [../TESTING.md](../TESTING.md). The flow control and the long transfers of an ISO15765 channel: [j2534fc.md](j2534fc.md). The layouts: sections 5, 8.1 and 13 of the wire protocol document in the components repository (`components/j2534_server/J2534_WIRE_PROTOCOL.md`).

## What it covers

Five defects of the J2534 server, found by reading its code on 2026-11-18 and shown on this bench before they were fixed (the first record is the red run):

1. **The PC vanishes.** A TCP tester that disappeared without closing (a pulled cable, a laptop asleep) kept the one session until the unit restarted, so no tool could connect again: the send blocked for good and nothing asked whether the peer was still there. The connection now carries TCP keepalive (the first probe after 10 s of silence, one every 5 s, given up after 3) and a send timeout of 5 s.
2. **Messages are lost and nobody is told.** A channel's receive queue holds 256 bus frames. When it runs over, the oldest frame goes for every new one, and the tool was never told. A client that speaks wire version 2 now gets an `EVENT RX_LOST` with the count, in front of the first message behind the gap; the Windows driver (1.10) turns it into SAE J2534-1's `ERR_BUFFER_OVERFLOW`.
3. **A long ISO 15765 write held everything up.** The write held the one channel lock from its first frame to its last. The RX pump waited on it: nothing received on any channel reached the tool meanwhile, and what did not fit a queue was lost.
4. **Periodic messages ran in the unit's timer task.** They waited on that same lock inside the esp_timer task, which serves every software timer of the unit, and afterwards the timer fired once for every period it had missed: the copies went out in one burst. A task of its own sends them now. In the same table: a periodic message stayed in it when its channel was disconnected and went out again on the next channel that was connected; `DISCONNECT` now ends the channel's periodic messages, as SAE J2534-1 7.3.6 has it.
5. **The return codes.** Seven were numbered wrong against SAE J2534-1, in the firmware and in the driver alike (`ERR_INVALID_BAUDRATE` 0x18 where the standard has 0x19, `ERR_DEVICE_IN_USE` 0x1A where it has 0x0E, and so on). A client that asks for wire version 2 in its `HELLO` gets the standard's numbers; every other client gets what it always got.

The PCAN plays an ECU that takes a long request slowly (its flow control asks for 10 ms between frames, so a 1400-byte request is on the bus for about 2.2 s), is the witness of the periodic messages (their bus timestamps) and makes the traffic (frames with a counter in their first four bytes). Three things need a client one TCP hop from the unit, which a client behind the ssh tunnel is not: a connection whose packets are dropped both ways, so that neither a FIN nor a reset reaches the unit; a client with a small receive buffer that stops reading; a client that is silent for half a minute. They run on the Pi: `tools/testbench/usb/j2534_session_pi.py` is copied to `/tmp` there, driven through ssh with one JSON line per command, and removed at the end. It drops the packets of its own connection with a table of its own (`inet j2534_session_bench`: the client's source port to the unit's port 6809 and back), removed in a `finally`, at its start and by the stage.

## What it does not cover

- A real PC that vanishes. The packets of one connection are dropped on the Pi, which is the same from the unit's side: no segment of the peer arrives any more. A PC that comes back after its sleep with the old connection is not run (its first segment is answered with a reset once the unit has given the session up).
- The USB serial and the Bluetooth transport. Keepalive and the send timeout are the TCP listener's; a serial session ends with DTR and a Bluetooth one with its link. The refusal of a second tester on a second transport (`ERR_DEVICE_IN_USE`, 0x1A or 0x0E by the version asked) is not a leg here: `.\test.ps1 blej2534` runs it at wire version 1 (its L5, a TCP tester while Bluetooth holds the session, answered 0x1A), the host tests hold the bytes of both answers, and D1's fake devices are the driver's side of it. A tester at wire version 2 refused by the unit itself (0x0E) is not run on any bench.
- A TP2.0 and a J1850 write. Both still hold the channel lock to their end (the TP2.0 write reads the channel's queue itself), so what leg L shows for ISO15765 does not hold for them: received messages of other channels and periodic messages wait for such a write. A periodic message of more than one frame on an ISO15765 channel holds the lock for its transfer too.
- A periodic message to the ECU a multi-frame write is talking to (it waits for the transfer's end by design, SAE J2534-1: no periodic message into a segmented transfer to the same address): leg L's periodic message goes to another id.
- Losses that are not a channel's bus queue: a message the ISO-TP provider had no room for (its own counter, `provider_stats.rx_dropped` on `/api/uds`), the J1850 and TP2.0 delivery rings.
- What a real application does with `ERR_BUFFER_OVERFLOW`. The standard calls the messages of that call valid; leg D reads them through the DLL the way an application would.
- 29-bit ids, a bus at another speed than 500 kbit/s.

## Traps

- `WICAN_PCAN_DUT` must name the PCAN channel on the DUT's bus in the same PowerShell command (`$env:WICAN_PCAN_DUT = 'PCAN_USBBUS2'` on this bench). On the wrong channel no ECU hears the long request and legs L and O fail from their first check.
- **Never ask for another bit rate on a bus whose bit rate is not proven.** Three of the refusals in legs N and D come from a `CONNECT` at 250000. With `can_manager.baud = auto` and nothing read off the wire yet, on a silent bus, the firmware TAKES a tester's bit rate instead of refusing it (2026-10-17), and the unit would then sit at 250 kbit/s on this bench's 500 kbit/s bus. N0 therefore makes the PCAN talk first and reads `/api/can`: the legs run only when the node is at 500 kbit/s, fixed or verified.
- **The packet filter on the Pi.** The helper drops the packets of its own connection in a table of its own, `inet j2534_session_bench` (two rules: its source port to the unit's port 6809, and back). It removes the table in a `finally`, again when it starts, and the stage removes it once more. If a run was killed and a J2534 client on the Pi cannot reach the unit: `sudo nft delete table inet j2534_session_bench`. Nothing else on the Pi is touched (NetworkManager's own table stays).
- **On a firmware without the fix, legs V do not leave the session held.** The helper removes its rules first and closes its sockets after, so the unit gets the FIN and frees the session; the red run's V2 and V4 ("a new session is served after it") pass for that reason. A helper that is killed in between leaves the session held until the unit restarts, which the stage's restore does when the J2534 server was off as found.
- **Leg V1's 25 s counts from the peer's last segment**: 10 s of silence, then three probes 5 s apart. The helper measures from the moment its rules are in place to the answer to a `HELLO` it sent at that moment on a second connection (which waited in the unit's listen queue), so 25.4 s is the session's end plus the next session's start.
- **Leg O's numbers move from run to run.** Where the gaps fall depends on what the two TCP buffers hold (the unit's 20 KB send buffer; the Pi's receive buffer, 4 KB asked for and doubled by Linux) and on whether the unit's blocked send got one batch through in the middle of the stall (it did in every run so far, hence two gaps, 32 messages apart). The checks compare the events with the gaps of the same run. `dispatch_drops` on `/api/can` is the sum over every reader of the CAN node, so O1 asks for at least the missing frames; O4 and O5 are the exact ones.
- **Leg O stalls for 2.5 s, under the unit's send timeout of 5 s.** A client that does not read for more than 5 s while the unit has data for it is dropped like a dead one (leg V3 is that case).
- **L3's limit is the bench link**, not the unit: the frames go from the bus over WiFi and the ssh tunnel to the client, 0.05 to 0.15 s in these runs. On a firmware without the fix the same frames wait for the end of the write (1.2 s for the slowest that survived).
- **The ECU of leg L counts bytes**, it does not check ISO 15765-2 sequence numbers; the request is compared whole when it is complete. Its flow control is `30 00 0A` (no block limit, 10 ms).
- **Leg D against a driver before 1.10 fails D1 and D2 by design** (it asks for wire version 1, passes the old numbers through and reports no loss). D3 with `--old-dll` is the leg for such a driver. `j2534_session_dll_check.py --fake` needs neither the unit nor the bench: it is the check to run on a driver build before its installer is made.
- **One session only.** While the stage runs no other J2534 client can attach to the unit, and a client that is attached when it starts makes leg A fail at its first request.
