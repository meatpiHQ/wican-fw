**Leg S, a client that stalls (2026-11-17).** Born from a field report on 6.0.0-beta.11: slcan on port 3333 with a bus at about 1,100 frames a second closed on an Android phone in bursts, about once a minute while driving, and held on a laptop; a clean close, the reconnect worked at once. Reproduced with that image: a client whose ACKs came 166 ms late was closed at every gap, 127 ms late never. The cause: the server closed a client whose `send()` did not finish in 100 ms, and with one small write per CAN frame lwIP's queue of 57 unacknowledged writes is 52 ms of that bus. The fix and its reasons: `socket_manager/README.md`, "A client that stalls". The leg runs with the two connection rows of leg B in place; PCAN sends 1,100 frames a second (a 32-bit counter in the data) and `tools/testbench/can/slcan_stall_client.py` on the Pi is the client. It connects from one of 64 local ports and holds only its own packets back (`tc` netem behind a prio qdisc and a u32 filter) or drops them (`nft`), so the other clients on the Pi and the web API keep their timing. S1 the control: 10 s without a stall, every frame, nothing dropped. S2, S3 the ACKs late by 300 ms and by 600 ms every 3 s: no close, no frame lost, `tx_drops` and the controller's `dispatch_drops` + `rx_missed` unchanged. S4 late for longer than the client buffer holds (2.5 s against the 32 KB of before 2026-11-18; since then one and a half times what the buffer, read from `/api/sockets` `client_buffer_bytes`, and lwIP's 20 KB hold of this bus: 5.3 s with the 64 KB of that day): the client stays, the frames it lost are counted in `tx_drops` and said in the log (`a client is behind`, `the client caught up`), none lost before the server. S5 a second client on the PC that reads all the time loses nothing while the Pi's client is 2.5 s behind. S6 the Pi's ACKs dropped for 15 s: the client is closed after 10 s with `client dropped (took no data for 10 s, ...)`, and the next client receives. Needs `sudo -n` for `tc` and `nft` on the Pi (SKIP with the reason otherwise).

**The unit's own search for its home network, by hand (2026-11-18, not a leg of the stage).** The reporter's settings: access point and station together, one home network, which is out of reach in the car. The unit then keeps trying that network: every 30 s when the station has not been connected since the start (`AP has 1 clients but no STA session yet this boot: connecting anyway`), about every 65 s when it has (the pause for a client on the access point, then `the pause budget is spent: connecting anyway`). Each try is a scan of all channels and silences the access point three times for about 365 ms; now and then a packet is lost with it and the stall is 1.2 to 1.5 s. Measured with the Pi's station radio as the client on the unit's access point, the bench's home network switched off, 1,100 frames a second, 14 minutes from a cold start, 28 tries each: **6.0.0-beta.11 release image, 17 closes** (`client dropped (stalled/dead on send)` one to two seconds after a try, in runs with quiet minutes between, 25,741 of 926,043 frames lost); **the build with the new send path (`43f123ff...`), no close by a stall**, four stalls of 1.2 to 1.5 s that ran over the 32 KB backlog (869 frames dropped and counted while connected), and one close after the Pi's own radio lost the access point for 11 s (its NetworkManager log has the disassociation; the server closed at 10 s without a byte, as designed). So the field report's closes while driving have a cause on the unit itself beside the phone's radio. A 5-minute run from a warm start (4 tries) had shown 0 closes on the release image: too short a sample, the long run is the one that counts. Tools: `internal/slcan_3333_drop/tools/ap_scan_run.py` and `ap_gap_client.py` (gitignored). Open: whether the unit should try less often while a client is on its access point, and whether the backlog should hold more than 1.5 s.

**The pace of that search as a setting, by hand (2026-11-18 08:52 to 09:12, image `f9e289cf...`).** `wifi_manager.sta_search_interval_s` (10 to 3600 s, 30 by default) is the slowest pace of the tries; `/api/wifi/status` reports it and counts the tries (`sta_attempt.search_interval_s`, `.tries`). The same set-up as above: the bench access point off, the unit restarted cold through its own access point, the Pi's second radio on that access point reading the count once a second (`internal/slcan_3333_drop/tools/search_pace.py`). With 120 s: the tries 80 s and then 120.1, 120.0, 120.0 and 120.0 s apart over twelve minutes. With 30 s: ten tries in five minutes, 26 to 35 s apart as a one-second poll through a stalling access point sees them. The bench access point back on, the unit was on it again after 12 s; the setting is back at 30.

Traps of the leg, each of which cost a run. Taking the netem qdisc away right behind the last gap throws the ACKs still waiting in it away, and the stall the unit then sees is its own retransmit timer, seconds long: the client waits a gap's length before it deletes the qdisc. A delay on the whole interface also delays the second client's tunnel and the web API: hence the filter on the client's own ports. A source port reused within two minutes of a connection the unit closed is answered late (the unit keeps the old pair in TIME_WAIT): the client takes its 64 ports in turn across runs (`/tmp/slcan_stall_port` on the Pi). `candump -n N` counts every frame on the bus, and a bench unit with AutoPID on has its own: leg L filters on the generator's id since. A unit with five connection rows of its own has no room for the stage's two: one is left out for the run (`br_cli`) and leg R puts it back. The `/ws/can` channel is off in the canonical bench settings: staged on with the rows.

**Leg B, the burst (B11, B12, 2026-11-18).** A client that writes many frames in one go. Found while testing leg S: of 400 slcan lines written at once 161 to 209 reached the bus, the first missing one the 22nd to 26th, on 6.0.0-beta.11 and on the builds of that night alike (20 lines: all). The controller holds 16 frames in flight and refuses one more at once (`can_drv_transmit`, no counter on `/api/can` for it), and the bridge dropped the frames behind a refused one in the same batch; `/api/bridges` counted the refusals as `send_errors`. Three changes in meatpi-components: the `can` jack puts a client's frames into a queue of its own and a task of the jack hands them to the controller, waiting for room between frames (`can_manager_tx_room`, up to 100 ms); the bridge's pump leaves a client's data queued while that queue has no room (`tx_ready`); and a TCP server does not read from its clients while its bridge's queue is full, so the client's TCP holds its sender back (without the last the loss only moves into `rx_drops`). B11 writes 400 lines and B12 2000 lines in one `sendall` through the tunnel (id 0x326, a 32-bit counter); PCAN must see every counter once, in order, and `send_errors`, `codec_errors`, the server's `rx_drops`, the controller's `tx_refused` and `tx_lost` must not move, `tx` by at least the count.

**B13, B14: the burst while the bus streams to the same client (2026-11-18).** The first build of this fix waited for the bus inside the bridge's pump, which serves both directions of a connection. It passed B11 and B12 and failed what nobody had measured: 2000 lines written into a bus that streamed to the same client cost that client 162 of the stream's frames at 1,100 frames a second, 1,015 at 2,000 and 3,938 at 3,000 (0 without the burst), with no counter moving on any status route. Hence the queue and the task above: the pump no longer waits. B13 (1,100 frames a second) and B14 (2,000) write 2000 lines while PCAN streams and a reader on the same connection counts the stream: every line on the bus once, and no frame of the stream lost. The order of the burst is asserted too. The first runs showed it broken on a busy bus, by a defect of the CAN driver older than this change: a frame of ours that lost arbitration was handed to the node driver again at the queue's own priority and went out behind up to 15 frames written after it (42 of 2000 out of place at 1,100 frames a second, 349 at 2,000). The retry now carries a higher queue priority (`can_core_driver.c`, `tx_queue_priority`), so it leaves first.

**B15: the stream while the unit writes its flash (2026-11-18).** A flash erase stops every task for about 23 ms; the frames of that time wait in the CAN node's receive queue (256 frames since 2026-11-17) and then reach the `can` jack at once, whose own queue had stayed at 32. By hand, three files of 32 KB uploaded while an slcan client read the bus: 0 lost at 1,100 frames a second, 384 at 2,000, 886 at 3,000, each counted in `dispatch_drops` on `/api/can`. The jack's queue holds 256 since. B15 streams 2,000 frames a second, uploads three files of 32 KB to `/data` meanwhile (the erase counter must move, else the check tested nothing) and asserts that the client lost no frame and that `dispatch_drops` and `rx_missed` stood still.
