**slcan over TCP, and the trap first** (prints `SLCAN BRIDGE PASS`, ~3 min, 2 to 3 planned restarts; born from a Discord report of 2026-10-16: "SLCAN works on the CAN monitor, not over python-can; socket_manager says client connected, nothing arrives", reproduced on the bench as the `slcan0` server switched on under Socket servers with no `can <-slcan-> slcan0` connection row). Leg T, the trap: that state (the as-found bench state, else staged with one restart): `/api/sockets` says `bridge: null`, a raw client with python-can's opening bytes reads nothing in 3.5 s while PCAN puts 20 frames on the bus and `/api/can` counts them, and the log ring gains `W socket_manager: slcan0: no connection uses this server` at the connect. Leg B, bridged (one restart: `br_slcan = can <-slcan-> slcan0` and `br_can = can <-slcan-> ws_can`, any bridge on ws_can parked): `bridge` names both, python-can over the tunnel receives exactly N (200) frames in order while the /ws/can client beside it receives the same N, python-can's TX and a WS TX land on PCAN, both bridges pumped the same chunk count (the `can` jack fan-out), 0 drops at the server, the bridges and the controller. Leg L, Linux on rpi001 (SKIP with the reason when python-can or sudo is missing there): python-can straight at the DUT (exactly N/2 in order, TX on PCAN) and the manual's route (`socat pty,link=/dev/netcan0`, `slcand -o -c -s6`, `ip link set can0 up`: candump N/2 frames, cansend on PCAN). Leg R: settings back as found (one restart), boot_count moved by the planned restarts only, unexpected resets and E lines unchanged. python-can 4.6.1 sits on rpi001 since 2026-10-16 (`~/.local`, installed from wheels: pypi is unreachable from the Pi).

**Leg S, a client that stalls (2026-11-17).** Born from a field report on 6.0.0-beta.11: slcan on port 3333 with a bus at about 1,100 frames a second closed on an Android phone in bursts, about once a minute while driving, and held on a laptop; a clean close, the reconnect worked at once. Reproduced with that image: a client whose ACKs came 166 ms late was closed at every gap, 127 ms late never. The cause: the server closed a client whose `send()` did not finish in 100 ms, and with one small write per CAN frame lwIP's queue of 57 unacknowledged writes is 52 ms of that bus. The fix and its reasons: `socket_manager/README.md`, "A client that stalls". The leg runs with the two connection rows of leg B in place; PCAN sends 1,100 frames a second (a 32-bit counter in the data) and `tools/testbench/can/slcan_stall_client.py` on the Pi is the client. It connects from one of 64 local ports and holds only its own packets back (`tc` netem behind a prio qdisc and a u32 filter) or drops them (`nft`), so the other clients on the Pi and the web API keep their timing. S1 the control: 10 s without a stall, every frame, nothing dropped. S2, S3 the ACKs late by 300 ms and by 600 ms every 3 s: no close, no frame lost, `tx_drops` and the controller's `dispatch_drops` + `rx_missed` unchanged. S4 late by 2.5 s, longer than the 32 KB backlog holds: the client stays, the frames it lost are counted in `tx_drops` and said in the log (`a client is behind`, `the client caught up`), none lost before the server. S5 a second client on the PC that reads all the time loses nothing while the Pi's client is 2.5 s behind. S6 the Pi's ACKs dropped for 15 s: the client is closed after 10 s with `client dropped (took no data for 10 s, ...)`, and the next client receives. Needs `sudo -n` for `tc` and `nft` on the Pi (SKIP with the reason otherwise).

Traps of the leg, each of which cost a run. Taking the netem qdisc away right behind the last gap throws the ACKs still waiting in it away, and the stall the unit then sees is its own retransmit timer, seconds long: the client waits a gap's length before it deletes the qdisc. A delay on the whole interface also delays the second client's tunnel and the web API: hence the filter on the client's own ports. A source port reused within two minutes of a connection the unit closed is answered late (the unit keeps the old pair in TIME_WAIT): the client takes its 64 ports in turn across runs (`/tmp/slcan_stall_port` on the Pi). `candump -n N` counts every frame on the bus, and a bench unit with AutoPID on has its own: leg L filters on the generator's id since. A unit with five connection rows of its own has no room for the stage's two: one is left out for the run (`br_cli`) and leg R puts it back. The `/ws/can` channel is off in the canonical bench settings: staged on with the rows.
