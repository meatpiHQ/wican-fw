## New since beta.11
- **VW TP 2.0 as a J2534 protocol of its own (`TP2_0_PS`).** A J2534 application can now open a TP 2.0 channel (SAE J2534-2, protocol id 0x800E) and leave the transport to WiCAN. The device sets the connection up (`REQUEST_CONNECTION`), splits a message into telegrams and puts a received one together, acknowledges blocks, sends again what the ECU asks for again, and keeps the connection alive with a connection test once a second, as SAE J2819 describes it. Up to four connections at once, messages of up to 4092 bytes, broadcast requests, and `GET_DEVICE_INFO` to ask what the device supports. Until now a tool had to do the transport itself on a raw CAN channel, with one round trip to the PC for every block acknowledgement, which is what made a TP 2.0 reflash slow over WiFi. The application supplies every data byte of a message, including the two length bytes a VW ECU expects in front of it, and reads the ECU's answer with its two length bytes in front. Tested against a model of an ECU that follows SAE J2819 and against a published exchange of a real engine ECU, not yet on a car. From Windows it needs driver 1.8.0 (the next entry): driver 1.7 does not know the protocol. Contributed by @EliasKotlyar ([meatpi-components #4](https://github.com/meatpiHQ/meatpi-components/pull/4)).
- **Windows J2534 driver 1.8.0.** The installer in [drivers/j2534](https://github.com/meatpiHQ/wican-fw/tree/dev-v6/drivers/j2534) is updated. It adds the TP 2.0 protocol of the entry above (`TP2_0_PS`): the DLL hands a TP 2.0 channel to the WiCAN and passes messages through untouched, `GET_DEVICE_INFO` tells an application what the device supports, and a write to an id without a connection returns `ERR_NO_CONNECTION_ESTABLISHED`. TP 2.0 needs this firmware version or later; everything else in the driver works with the firmware driver 1.7 worked with. One change reaches every tool: on CAN, ISO15765 and J1850 VPW channels `PassThruReadMsgs` now returns the messages it has once nothing more arrived for 4 ms, where it used to wait for the full count until its timeout. A flasher that asks for a whole TP 2.0 block in one read on a raw CAN channel waited out that timeout at every block end; a read that got nothing still waits its timeout. The installer also writes a `TP2_0` registry value beside `CAN` and `ISO15765`. Contributed by @EliasKotlyar ([wican-j2534-driver #2](https://github.com/meatpiHQ/wican-j2534-driver/pull/2)).

## Bug fixes since beta.11
- **A rule that posts to an `https://` address restarted the device.** A rule on the Automate page whose action is `http.post` to an `https://` URL, for example a bump posted to a Home Assistant webhook, restarted WiCAN the first time it fired, and the crash report read `A stack overflow in task em_worker`. Three such restarts in a row, each within ten minutes of its start, park the device (LED breathing red), so a rule that fires often could take a device out. The task that runs a rule's network actions had 4 KB of stack, and the encrypted connection of an https post needs about 4.5 KB. A post to a plain `http://` address fitted, and so did a post that failed before the connection was made (no network yet), which is why a rule could seem to work at first. The task now has 16 KB, of which an https post leaves about 12 KB unused. Reported on 6.0.0-beta.10 and reproduced on 6.0.0-beta.11. Until you update: let the rule publish over MQTT (`mqtt.publish`), or post to a plain `http://` address on your own network. (#1067)
