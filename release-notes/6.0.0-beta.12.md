## New since beta.11
- **VW TP 2.0 as a J2534 protocol of its own (`TP2_0_PS`).** A J2534 application can now open a TP 2.0 channel (SAE J2534-2, protocol id 0x800E) and leave the transport to WiCAN. The device sets the connection up (`REQUEST_CONNECTION`), splits a message into telegrams and puts a received one together, acknowledges blocks, sends again what the ECU asks for again, and keeps the connection alive with a connection test once a second, as SAE J2819 describes it. Up to four connections at once, messages of up to 4092 bytes, broadcast requests, and `GET_DEVICE_INFO` to ask what the device supports. Until now a tool had to do the transport itself on a raw CAN channel, with one round trip to the PC for every block acknowledgement, which is what made a TP 2.0 reflash slow over WiFi. The application supplies every data byte of a message, including the two length bytes a VW ECU expects in front of it, and reads the ECU's answer with its two length bytes in front. Tested against a model of an ECU that follows SAE J2819 and against a published exchange of a real engine ECU, not yet on a car. It needs a Windows driver that knows the protocol: driver 1.7 does not, and the driver that does is not released yet. Contributed by @EliasKotlyar ([meatpi-components #4](https://github.com/meatpiHQ/meatpi-components/pull/4)).

## Bug fixes since beta.11
- **A rule that posts to an `https://` address restarted the device.** A rule on the Automate page whose action is `http.post` to an `https://` URL, for example a bump posted to a Home Assistant webhook, restarted WiCAN the first time it fired, and the crash report read `A stack overflow in task em_worker`. Three such restarts in a row, each within ten minutes of its start, park the device (LED breathing red), so a rule that fires often could take a device out. The task that runs a rule's network actions had 4 KB of stack, and the encrypted connection of an https post needs about 4.5 KB. A post to a plain `http://` address fitted, and so did a post that failed before the connection was made (no network yet), which is why a rule could seem to work at first. The task now has 16 KB, of which an https post leaves about 12 KB unused. Reported on 6.0.0-beta.10 and reproduced on 6.0.0-beta.11. Until you update: let the rule publish over MQTT (`mqtt.publish`), or post to a plain `http://` address on your own network. (#1067)
